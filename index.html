Frequently asked questions

Use this audiobook speed calculator for fixed-length podcasts, YouTube videos, and course recordings when you know the original duration and playback speed. Player speed limits differ across platforms, devices, and versions. Estimates cover one uninterrupted listen and exclude pauses, rewinds, ads, and skipped sections.

How much time do 1.25× and 1.35× speeds save?

The savings percentage is (1 − 1 ÷ speed) × 100%. At 1.25×, that is 20%: a 7-hour audiobook takes 5 hours 36 minutes, saving 1 hour 24 minutes. At 1.35×, it is about 25.93%: a 3-hour audiobook takes 2 hours 13 minutes 20 seconds, saving 46 minutes 40 seconds. At either speed, this audiobook speed calculator rounds displayed estimates up to the next second.

How long are 9-hour and 11-hour audiobooks at 1.5×?

At 1.5×, a 9-hour audiobook takes 6 hours, saving 3 hours. An 11-hour audiobook takes 7 hours 20 minutes, saving 3 hours 40 minutes. If you have already listened, subtract your position in the original book from its full duration, then divide the remainder by speed. Progress measures the book position, not the time spent listening.

Does listening at 2× affect comprehension?

Comprehension varies with the material, narration, language familiarity, and listener. A UCLA study of recorded class lectures found little difference in tested comprehension up to 2×, while results were lower at 2.5×; it does not predict the same outcome for audiobooks or every person. Try a comfortable speed and slow down for dense passages. The audiobook speed calculator estimates duration, not individual comprehension.

The Audible app lets you choose Speed in the player, while exact controls may vary by device or version. For comparison, the audiobook speed calculator accepts a custom number; that does not mean Audible offers every possible value.

How do I calculate time left and listening progress?

Time left = (original duration − original progress) ÷ playback speed. Progress percentage = original progress ÷ original duration × 100%. For a 10-hour book with 2 hours heard, progress is 20%; at 1.5×, the remaining 8 hours take 5 hours 20 minutes. The audiobook speed calculator treats progress as book position and divides only the remaining duration.

How do audiobook hours convert to pages?

A page count is only a rough estimate: original hours × 60 × words per minute ÷ words per page. With 150 spoken words per minute and 300 words per page, a 2-hour audiobook is about 60 pages. Editions, layout, and narration vary. For a page estimate, restore the book's original duration first; an audiobook speed calculator cannot identify a specific edition's pagination.

Can I use this calculator for podcasts, YouTube, and courses?

Yes, if the podcast, YouTube video, or course recording has a known duration and the player offers the speed you want. Enter its 1× duration and actual playback setting. The calculator assumes one uninterrupted listen; it cannot predict live streams or add pauses, rewinds, ads, or skipped segments. This audiobook speed calculator can estimate fixed-length audio or video.

Does changing playback speed change the pitch?

Some audio players preserve pitch while changing speed; others may let pitch rise or fall. The setting and audio processing depend on the player, and very high rates can sound distorted. Browser media documentation describes pitch preservation as a separate playback setting, but that does not guarantee identical behavior in audiobook apps. An audiobook speed calculator divides duration; it does not process audio.

Why does my result differ from my player's time display?

Check that the original duration, playback speed, and position match, and that both displays refer to the same scope, such as a whole title or one chapter. An audiobook speed calculator rounds estimates up to the next second; a player's live countdown may also reflect pauses, silence skipping, ads, and chapter boundaries. Rewinds can further change the result from a simple full-duration estimate.

Is an audiobook membership cheaper than buying individual books?

Compare actual costs for your listening pattern: divide the membership fee by the number of books you finish, then compare that per-book figure with individual purchase prices. Consider included titles, credits, discounts, and how access changes after cancellation. Audible says purchased titles remain accessible, while included-catalog access and unused credits may end. Plan prices and benefits vary by region and can change, so compare current local terms before deciding.
